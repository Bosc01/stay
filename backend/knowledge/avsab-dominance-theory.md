Dominance is a relationship between two animals established by force and submission
that decides who gets first access to a resource. It is not an explanation for most
behavior owners want to change.

Excessive barking, unruly greetings, and failure to come when called are not about
valued resources and often do not involve aggression at all. They happen because the
behavior has been inadvertently rewarded and because no alternative behavior was
taught. What the owner actually needs is influence, not rank.

Fear and anxiety are common causes of aggression and of other behavior problems,
including ones that look like resource guarding. Punishment can suppress the outward
aggression without touching the cause, and it can directly make the problem worse by
raising the dog's fear or anxiety.

Forcing a dog into submission to correct a behavior problem is not supported. Dogs
that are less sure of their position show more aggressive display, not less, so a
confrontational approach tends to escalate rather than settle things.
