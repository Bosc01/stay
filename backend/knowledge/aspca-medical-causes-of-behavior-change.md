A behavior that is new, or that changed recently, can be a medical problem rather than
a training problem. Conditions named on the page include arthritis, dental disease,
hypothyroidism, cancer, failing sight or hearing, urinary tract disease, and Cushing's
disease. These can produce sensitivity to touch, anxiety about being handled, more
aggression, and less responsiveness.

Because many behavior changes are signs of a treatable medical disorder, a thorough
veterinary exam comes before behavior modification. The page warns against assuming a
dog is just getting old and nothing can be done.

Signs that point at pain or cognitive decline rather than misbehavior: confusion in
familiar places, a reversed sleep-wake cycle, house soiling in random spots or in the
dog's own sleeping area, and a drop in responsiveness. The page groups cognitive
dysfunction signs as confusion and disorientation, decreased responsiveness, changes in
activity, sleep disturbance, and loss of house training.
