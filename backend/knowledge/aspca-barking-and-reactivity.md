For barking and lunging at people or dogs, the first move is to cut the dog's exposure
to the trigger. Block the view with removable window film or a spray-on glass coating,
keep the dog away from the window or door where it happens, and use solid opaque
fencing outdoors.

Then desensitization and counterconditioning. Start at least eight feet from the
trigger, further if the dog reacts at eight. Use soft high-value food, chicken, cheese,
or hot dog. The treat arrives when the dog chooses not to bark: walking calmly past a
person or dog, or sitting while one passes. Build over many sessions, intensifying the
distraction gradually rather than closing the distance all at once.

Teach an alternative behavior that is incompatible with barking at the door, such as
going to a designated spot and lying down when a trigger appears.

Do not use punishment for fear-based barking. No yelling or scolding, no anti-bark
collars, which are punishment devices, and no muzzle for long periods, unsupervised, or
tied closed.
